Intended use

A controlled combat-modeling workbench

The simulator combines your equipment, upgrades, consumables, weapons, traits, skills, boons, and target settings into a browser-based PvE combat model. Queue skills in the rotation builder, then review the resulting timeline and damage breakdown.

Use it to compare builds or rotations under identical assumptions, isolate why a result changed, validate profession implementations, and measure how RNG affects output. Results are modeled single-target estimates, not a replacement for in-game validation, encounter-specific testing, or benchmark logs.

What you can inspect

  • Build inputsGear, attributes, weapons, traits, and skills
  • Execution stateCasts, cooldowns, ammo, swaps, boons, and resources
  • Damage outputStrike, condition, skill, and modifier contributions
  • Outcome varianceMean, median, likely range, and rare RNG outcomes

Advanced simulation modes

Expected output and RNG distributions

Mode 01

Deterministic expected

The default mode produces the same detailed result for the same build and rotation. Weapon strength uses exact range midpoints, strike critical damage remains expected-valued, and supported random procs advance at their expected rate.

Use this baseline for event timelines, skill and condition breakdowns, modifier attribution, debugging, and controlled comparisons.

Mode 02

RNG distribution

This mode keeps the deterministic result for detailed inspection, then runs 500 reproducibly seeded outcomes in background workers. Trials sample weapon strength per activation and supported proc or on-critical behavior through isolated random streams.

The result reports mean and median DPS, the likely range, and unlucky and lucky outcomes without making a random seed part of the saved build.

Technical details

How a rotation becomes a result

01

Schedule the rotation

A discrete-event scheduler validates skill availability and places casts, cooldowns, ammo, swaps, profession actions, and deferred effects on a millisecond timeline.

02

Resolve combat state

A separate resolver drains that timeline in order, applying Guild Wars 2 strike and condition formulas, modifiers, target state, boons, resources, sigils, relics, and profession reactions.

03

Model profession rules

Common engine and Guild Wars 2 rules are shared, while each profession owns its skill catalog, build rules, resources, mechanic handlers, and simulation hooks.

04

Calculate in the browser

Simulation and analysis run client-side, with RNG trials partitioned across up to four Web Workers. Profession pages keep independent local builds and support portable JSON import and export.
